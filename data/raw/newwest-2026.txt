# NWP Launches 'New West Works' Economic Plan Focused on Affordability, Accountability, and Downtown Revitalization

## Strong local businesses make for stronger and more vibrant neighbourhoods.

New Westminster, B.C. (May 19, 2026) — The New West Progressives (NWP) today announced the launch of "New West Works," a new economic and affordability-focused policy initiative aimed at supporting local businesses, restoring fiscal accountability, revitalizing downtown, and strengthening New Westminster's economy.

The announcement follows a recent NWP business roundtable attended by local business owners and community stakeholders from across New Westminster who voiced growing concerns about rising costs, public safety challenges, declining competitiveness, and a lack of responsiveness from City Hall.

Council candidate Maryann Morrison said the feedback received from residents and business owners was both direct and consistent.

"People want practical leadership and a City Hall that listens," says Morrison. "Residents and local businesses are telling us they are worried about affordability, safety, economic uncertainty, and the overall direction of the city. 'New West Works' is about restoring confidence, supporting local investment, and making New Westminster more welcoming, vibrant, and economically resilient again."

The NWP says feedback received through its CityMatters community survey, stakeholder consultations, and business engagement sessions helped shape the initiative.

Initial "New West Works" commitments include:

- Eliminating the $2.1 million electricity surtax (3.5%) currently charged only to New Westminster residents ($1.1M) and businesses ($1M) through the New Westminster Electrical Utility. BC Hydro customers are not subjected to the same punitive tax on green energy.
- Creating a new Financial Accountability and Budget Oversight Committee to strengthen transparency and improve public confidence in City finances
- Reducing external consultant spending by at least 20% within the first two years compared to the 2026 baseline budget
- Permanently eliminating all patio permit fees and setting a goal of increasing patio seating capacity citywide by 25% over the next term of office
- Introducing a new lower-cost three-year business license option to reduce red tape and requirements for annual renewals
- Eliminating business license application fees and address change fees
- Piloting a new information sharing agreement with the New Westminster Chamber of Commerce to strengthen local business connections and support services
- Cancelling the costly "Sue Big Oil" initiative and redirecting those funds toward practical local environmental priorities instead

New Westminster mayoral candidate and Councillor Daniel Fontaine said strong economic leadership and fiscal discipline are essential to protecting quality of life in the city.

"Strong local businesses make for stronger and more vibrant neighbourhoods," says Fontaine. "New Westminster can absolutely be compassionate, environmentally responsible, and economically competitive at the same time. Residents should not have to choose between affordability, public safety, economic growth, and quality of life."

"The City needs to re-prioritize its agenda and start supporting local businesses and economic growth for a change," says Blair Goodman, owner of Eclipps Hair Cafe. "I'm fully behind the New West Works initiative put forward by NWP mayoral candidate Daniel Fontaine and his entire council team."

Additional policy announcements focused on affordability, economic development, public safety, infrastructure, transportation, housing, and quality of life will be released in the months ahead leading up to the October 17, 2026 civic election.

The NWP says "New West Works" will continue to focus on practical solutions that strengthen the local economy, support small businesses, improve downtown vitality, and restore confidence in City Hall decision-making.

# NWP launches plan for Safer, Cleaner, and More Welcoming Neighbourhoods

## Platform a comprehensive set of non-policing initiatives to bring New West streets back to life, prevent crime, and support the most vulnerable.

New Westminster, B.C. (June 22, 2026) — The New Westminster Progressives (NWP) have launched their Safer, Cleaner, and More Welcoming Neighbourhoods platform – a comprehensive set of non-policing initiatives that will increase the vibrancy of public spaces, enhance safety, support the most vulnerable in our city, and clean things up.

"Taken together, these initiatives will bring our streets and public spaces back to life and make them safe again," says Daniel Fontaine, NWP mayoral candidate and current New Westminster councillor. "As a team, we have the courage to do the hard things that must get done, which includes not just increased enforcement but also bringing back public music and art, focusing on the basics of cleaning and lighting, and supporting our most vulnerable citizens."

Safer, Cleaner, and More Welcome Neighbourhoods platform:

1. Place at least 12 new Community Enforcement and Safety Officers (CESO) on foot and bicycle patrols in the Downtown, Uptown, Sapperton and Queensborough. They will be a presence in these neighbourhoods at times including evenings and weekends to enhance public safety, increase by-law infraction enforcement and support local businesses. A revitalized Community Policing Office in Queensborough will be assigned a permanent full-time CESO.
2. Triple the City's investment in the successful I's On the Street program, an initiative by the New Westminster Homeless Coalition Society that provides paid street-cleaning employment for people who are unhoused or at risk of homelessness. Focused on the Downtown and Uptown neighbourhoods, this investment will further help vulnerable individuals gain job skills and community connections – while helping to ensure our streets are clean.
3. Work with the New Westminster Police Department and Fraser Health Authority to explore the opportunity to introduce a Car 87-type program to better meet the needs of individuals who are living with a mental illness. Vancouver's Car 87 program is a collaboration between the Vancouver Police Department and Vancouver Coastal Health that pairs police officers and nurses to respond to individuals experiencing mental health crisis.
4. Launch the VIP (Vibrancy Invites People) initiative to bring more buskers, public art, performances and other cultural activities to key commercial streets and public spaces, creating more activity on the street and helping make our neighbourhoods safer and more vibrant.
5. As part of the VIP initiative, pilot new lower cost micro-retail opportunities using decoratively wrapped shipping containers along key locations on Columbia Street. This will add more 'eyes on the street' and offer local retailers a cost-effective portal on to 'The Golden Mile'.
6. Enhance regular street cleaning and add 100 new waste and recycling bins in busy pedestrian areas.
7. Insist that TransLink and key stakeholders keep the New Westminster & Columbia SkyTrain stations clean and secure, making them a welcoming gateway to those entering our city via rapid transit.
8. Establish a new TAG Team to dramatically reduce graffiti by requiring the removal of hate-based and offensive graffiti on public property within 4 hours and all other graffiti within 72 hours.
9. Increase lighting to improve safety and visibility in key public spaces within high density neighbourhoods.
10. Establish a new Crime Reduction and Public Safety Task Force to coordinate action and deliver results.
11. The Mayor will seek appointment as Chair of the New Westminster Police Board.

"Our city government must be honest with itself about the state of our public spaces," says Paul Minhas, a New West business-owner who is running for re-election with NWP. "It is time for action – firm, yes, but also compassionate and in coordination with our police and local non-profit organizations doing tremendous work in our community."

The NWP is running 13 candidates for New Westminster city council and school board in the upcoming municipal election.

# NWP launches Active New West plan for better recreation, parks and sports facilities

## More places to play. More opportunities to participate.

New Westminster, B.C. (July 6, 2026) —  The New Westminster Progressives (NWP) have launched their Active New West platform — a plan for more places to play, more opportunities to participate, and stronger connections in a dense and growing city.

New Westminster is growing rapidly and is now Canada's second densest city. To remain livable and vibrant, residents need welcoming public spaces, affordable recreation opportunities, strong parks, reliable sports fields and facilities that keep pace with the needs of families, seniors, athletes and neighbourhoods across the city.

The NWP says New Westminster has strong foundations to build on, including cherished parks, active sports organizations and a waterfront with enormous potential. Active New West is focused on turning years of planning into practical improvements residents can see, use and enjoy.

**Enough talk. Let's get building.**

"New Westminster is dense and growing fast, but our recreation infrastructure has not kept up," says Daniel Fontaine, NWP mayoral candidate and current New Westminster councillor. "Families should not be fighting for field time, kids should not be stuck on waitlists because there are not enough places to play, and residents should not have to watch another study gather dust while facilities age. Our team is ready to move from talk to action — with a clear plan to build, repair and expand the parks, fields, arenas and public spaces our city needs."

NWP's Active New West plan is about moving beyond studies and delivering practical improvements residents can see and use — on fields, in parks, at recreation centres and along the waterfront.

"Recreation is not a nice-to-have — it is part of what makes a city livable," says Ray Porcellato, an NWP council candidate and president and GM of the New Westminster Salmonbellies. "Whether it is a child trying out for a team, a senior joining a program, a family walking the waterfront, or neighbours gathering in a local park, people need accessible, affordable places to be active and connected. Active New West is about getting the basics right, making better use of the spaces we have, and building the facilities our growing city has been waiting for."

**The NWP plan:**

1. Get New West to the front of the line for provincial and federal funding for new recreation infrastructure. We will:
   - Make a third indoor playing surface a top priority, with a clear, costed proposal.
   - Create a pipeline of shovel-ready recreation projects for a broad range of activities and sports, with clear timelines for priority projects.
   - Assertively pursue and secure provincial and federal funding.

2. Reinvent the waterfront as one of the most active, vibrant public spaces in Metro Vancouver. We will:
   - Finally rebuild the pier that burned down in 2020.
   - Incorporate fitness and recreation opportunities for adults into waterfront improvements.
   - Enhance walking and cycling connections between the Queensborough, Downtown and Sapperton waterfronts.
   - Create new opportunities for community events and fun at the waterfront.

3. Ensure existing sports fields can be used year-round, while pursuing new fields. We will:
   - Improve drainage on existing fields, so they can be used when it's raining.
   - Accelerate existing turf field conversions at Ryall Park and complete one additional turf field by 2030.
   - Work with sports groups to identify future needs and fast-track plans for new fields and covered outdoor playing surface areas.

4. Protect and expand park space as New Westminster grows. We will:
   - Require new developments and community plans to protect existing parkland and deliver more usable, accessible public space.
   - Ensure existing parks are better maintained.
   - Prioritize new park opportunities in underserved neighbourhoods with identified gaps.
   - Use every practical tool available, including land acquisition, partnerships, pocket parks, plazas and street-to-public-space conversions.
   - Secure funding to construct a unique Adventure Park in the city, with the preferred location being the təməsew̓txʷ Aquatic and Community Centre.

5. Keep recreation accessible and affordable. We will:
   - Expand access and programs for youth and seniors.
   - Explore parking fees for non-residents at recreation facilities, and earmark 100 per cent of any new revenue to keeping recreation affordable for New Westminster residents.
   - Make the capital investments in təməsew̓txʷ Aquatic and Community Centre needed to make it more family-friendly.

6. Create Active Streets neighbourhood festivals. We will support the launch of a summer 2027 pilot project along cycling and walking corridors that connect Sapperton, Downtown and Queensborough. We will:
   - Support more food trucks and local vendors, live street entertainment, family activities and community programming.
   - Partner with Tourism New Westminster, local businesses and business improvement areas that want to be involved.
   - Promote the events across Metro Vancouver, drawing people to our city.

7. Create a new Amateur Sport and Recreation Council, drawn from the city's sports organizations, seniors' recreation groups and accessibility advocates. People using our facilities should help shape their future.

# NWP releases Moving Forward New West transportation plan

## Common Sense Transportation – For Everyone: less gridlock, safer streets.

*New Westminster, B.C. (August 10, 2026)* — The New Westminster Progressives have released its transportation platform – *Moving Forward New West*. The plan offers common sense transportation for everyone, with specific actions that will reduce gridlock, improve safety and make it easier to move around the city.

Today, city residents deal with too much backed-up traffic, rail delays, train whistles, construction detours, missing sidewalks and parking rules that make simple trips harder. New Westminster sits at the centre of Metro Vancouver – a densely populated city and a major hub for traffic moving through the region and within our own neighbourhoods. That reality must be addressed with practical action.

"People are tired of waiting while congestion gets worse and basic problems go unsolved, and in fact made worse by City Hall," says Daniel Fontaine, NWP mayoral candidate and current New Westminster councillor. "A NWP council will focus on practical fixes that make life easier and respect every neighbourhood."

The plan includes regularly publishing a list of the city's 20 worst traffic bottlenecks and what's being done to address them, freeing up congestion on major connector routes and increasing bridge capacity, addressing rail delays and noise, a focus on pedestrians and transit, taking care of basics like sidewalks, protecting parking, and better traffic enforcement.

"Transportation should not be a culture war," says Ken Armstrong, NWP council candidate. "People walk, drive, cycle and take transit. They want common sense, safety, less delay and a City Hall that listens."

***The plan:***

1. **Fix New Westminster's worst traffic bottlenecks.** Residents don't want more studies – they want practical solutions. We will:
   - Identify and publish the City's 20 worst traffic bottlenecks within our first year.
   - Focus the city on addressing those bottlenecks with practical action.
   - Utilize modern technology to help prioritize practical intersection improvements including signal timing, turning lanes and traffic flow enhancements.
   - Publish an annual Traffic Improvement Report Card so residents can track our progress at these pain points.

2. **Bring Political Leadership Back to Rail Issues.** Railways are one of New Westminster's biggest transportation challenges and require consistent political leadership and advocacy. We will:
   - Immediately re-establish the Railway Community Advisory Panel (RCAP) – a joint group of elected officials, railway companies, City staff, emergency responders and community representatives. This was abandoned in 2022 by the current majority on Council.
   - Through RCAP address rail delays, emergency access, neighbourhood impacts and better coordinate long-term rail planning – including the replacement of the Queensborough rail swing bridge.
   - Commit to hold regular public meetings of RCAP with agendas, reports and action updates available to residents.

3. **Advocate to reduce train whistles.** Residents have waited long enough for action. We will:
   - Work with our local Member of Parliament, Transport Canada and railway companies to accelerate the implementation of additional rail quiet zones.
   - Seek partner funding and advocate for infrastructure improvements that reduce unnecessary train whistles while maintaining public safety.
   - Continue to provide regular public updates on progress toward quieter, whistle-free neighbourhoods.

4. **Champion major transportation investments.** New Westminster must have transportation infrastructure that keeps pace with our growing population. We will:
   - Secure a commitment from the Province of British Columbia to begin planning for the future of the Queensborough Bridge, including replacement, twinning or expansion options, as a provincial priority over the next decade.
   - Continue advocating for improved bus frequency and reliability throughout New Westminster, including enhanced service to Queensborough.
   - Work with senior governments to secure funding for major transportation projects and upgrades that improve mobility throughout our city – and reduce congestion.
   - Accelerate the installation of new bus shelters in high demand areas on high frequency bus routes. This includes the installation of new protective shelters directly adjacent to the NWSS.

5. **Deliver Common Sense Transportation Planning.** Transportation decisions should unite neighbourhoods – not divide them. We will:
   - Address congestion on connector routes to ensure traffic can move through the city quickly, and without resorting to driving through residential streets.
   - Ensure all major transportation projects are supported by meaningful – not performative – public consultation.
   - Require neighbourhood traffic impact assessments before significant road changes (including traffic calming) are approved.
   - Make all transportation decisions based on safety, evidence and community feedback – not political ideology.
   - Commit to undertaking a thorough internal review of the city's speed bump installation strategy & policies.
   - Undertake a 12-month pilot "30 Mins on Us" free parking program in the Columbia District to encourage more commerce and vibrancy downtown.
   - Work with the provincial government to finalize and plan and secure funding to provide free public transit for teens aged 13 to 18 in B.C.
   - Establish a new Transportation and Traffic citizen-based committee supported by City Hall.

6. **Take a balanced approach to cycling.** Cycling is an important part of our transportation network – but so are the needs of pedestrians, transit users, motorists and local businesses. We will:
   - Review the performance and efficacy of all existing bike lane projects to identify opportunities for improvement and cost savings.
   - Ensure future cycling investments are based on safety, evidence, public consultation, neighbourhood impacts.
   - Continue investing in recreational cycling initiatives, including initiatives that will connect Sapperton, Downtown and Queensborough waterfronts.
   - Work with the New Westminster Caucus and Southern Railway of British Columbia (SRY) to ensure the replacement of the Queensborough rail swing bridge incorporates new pedestrian and cycling infrastructure.

7. **Modernize Our transportation network.** Technology should help keep New Westminster moving. We will:
   - Expand smart traffic signal technology where appropriate.
   - Improve signal timing along major transportation corridors.
   - Use technology to reduce unnecessary delays and improve traffic flow throughout the city.

8. **Improve construction coordination.** Construction is necessary – but unnecessary disruption is not. We will:
   - Improve coordination of major construction projects – including those undertaken in our city by the Metro Vancouver Regional District.
   - Minimize unnecessary lane closures and traffic disruptions which can have major impacts on local businesses.
   - Increase fines for violations when new developments undertake unauthorized activities that impact local business operations.
   - Improve communication with residents and business owners through better – more advanced construction notices and detour information.

9. **Make Walking Safer and More Accessible.** Every trip begins and ends on foot. We will:
   - Prioritize missing sidewalk connections throughout the city – with a priority in Queensborough and Connaught Heights.
   - Improve pedestrian crossings near schools, parks, seniors' facilities and transit hubs. This includes a commitment to upgrade and improve safety at the 2nd St and 6th Ave intersection (within 12 months) due to its proximity to Ecole Herbert Spencer Elementary School.
   - Continue improving accessibility for residents of all ages and abilities.
   - Construct a 'scramble sidewalk' at the corner of 8th St and Carnarvon which currently experiences a high volume of pedestrian activity in 2027.
   - Improve pedestrian safety by significantly expanding and accelerating the implementation of 'pedestrian advance signals' in key areas throughout the city.
   - Provide long-term funding to the Society of Children and Youth BC to add at least two additional routes as part of their successful Walking School Bus program.

10. **Protect Neighbourhood Parking.** Parking policies should work for residents, businesses and visitors alike. We will:
    - Review residential parking policies to improve fairness, affordability and availability and provide more flexibility in the issuance of permits.
    - Improve wayfinding to public and private parking facilities.
    - Balance the parking needs of neighbourhoods, businesses and visitors when making all future capital investment decisions.
    - Within 100 days eliminate the city policy which is currently working to reduce the overall number of public parking spaces within the city.

11. **Prioritize Enforcement.** Streets are safer when reasonable traffic rules are enforced. We will:
    - Ensure Council works through the Police Board to emphasize the City's interest in supporting enhanced traffic enforcement and education programs throughout the city.
    - Utilize our existing by-laws and education programs to discourage the illegal usage of e-scooters, electric motor bikes etc…on city-owned sidewalks and other public places not under the jurisdiction of the NWPD.

**Enough gridlock. Let's get New Westminster moving again.**

# NWP launches plan to build complete communities

## More housing must come with parks, schools, services and infrastructure — and a City Hall that can keep up

*New Westminster, B.C. (July 13, 2026)* —  The New Westminster Progressives (NWP) have launched their Responsible Growth platform — a plan to build complete communities, modernize City Hall, and ensure growth improves residents' quality of life rather than diminishing it.

New Westminster has welcomed thousands of new residents over the past decade and is now Canada's second most densely populated city. Our community has played a central role in helping address Metro Vancouver's housing needs.

However, that housing growth has come without supporting infrastructure. The result is familiar to families and businesses across the city – traffic is backed up, park space per resident has declined, schools are crowded, recreation and cultural facilities have not kept pace, and permits take far too long to move through City Hall.

Enough talk. Let's build complete neighbourhoods.

> "New Westminster has done its share to help meet the region's housing needs," says Daniel Fontaine, NWP mayoral candidate and current New Westminster councillor. "But growth without infrastructure is not responsible growth. Residents have told us through New West City Matters that they want parks, schools, green space, jobs, transportation and community facilities that keep pace with development. Our plan is simple — build complete communities, protect what makes New West special, and make City Hall work better for residents and businesses."
>
> "Responsible growth means being thoughtful about where growth happens and making sure the basics are in place," says Rick Folka, NWP council candidate. "We should focus new housing in carefully selected neighbourhoods, especially around SkyTrain hubs, and advocate for Density Dollars so growth helps pay for the amenities and infrastructure residents need."

**The NWP Responsible Growth plan:**

**1. Build complete neighbourhoods** — not just more housing. Growth must come with the public amenities residents need. We will:

- Advocate for "Density Dollars" from senior governments so growth pays for new parks, schools, childcare, transportation improvements and community facilities.
- Focus growth in carefully selected neighbourhoods, especially around SkyTrain hubs, where transit, services and amenities can support new housing.
- Slow the pace of growth, where necessary, to allow infrastructure and public amenities to catch up.
- Prioritize new parks and green space in neighbourhoods experiencing the greatest growth.
- Ensure Community Amenity Contributions are invested primarily where growth is occurring.
- Introduce a Complete Communities Test before approving major neighbourhood plans.

**2. Protect neighbourhood character** while embracing thoughtful growth. Growth should strengthen our community — not erase what makes it special. We will:

- Support gentle density while respecting existing neighbourhood character.
- Continue supporting the Queen's Park Heritage Conservation Area.
- Work with neighbourhoods to establish additional Heritage Conservation Areas where there is demonstrated community support.
- Prioritize planning opportunities in communities like Connaught Heights, where residents have long advocated for additional housing choices.
- Protect industrial lands that support family-supporting jobs.

**3. Modernize City Hall**. Residents and businesses deserve faster approvals and better customer service. We will:

- Reduce permit approval times by at least 40 per cent during the next Council term.
- Publish quarterly online performance dashboards.
- Create a dedicated Permit Concierge to help applicants navigate the approval process.
- Expand online permitting, electronic inspections and real-time application tracking.

# NWP commits to protecting New Westminster's heritage as the city grows

## Heritage Plan: Distinctly New West will help protect the people, places, buildings, traditions and stories that make New Westminster unique

*New Westminster, B.C. (September 8, 2026)* — The New Westminster Progressives have released *Heritage Plan: Distinctly New West*, a commitment to protect New Westminster's heritage while welcoming new housing, new neighbours and continued growth.

"New Westminster has a strong sense of place, and that is worth protecting," says Karima Budhwani, NWP council candidate. "Our heritage is more than buildings. It includes our neighbourhoods, cultural institutions, civic traditions and the stories of the people and communities that helped shape this city."

The plan commits an NWP Council to deliver a comprehensive Heritage Plan within its first 18 months, modernize the City's approach to heritage preservation, strengthen resident input, recognize the full diversity of New Westminster's heritage and build on heritage as an economic asset.

"New Westminster can grow without losing what makes it distinct," says Daniel Fontaine, NWP mayoral candidate and current New Westminster councillor. "We can welcome new housing and new neighbours while carrying forward the history, character and community traditions that make this city special."

***The NWP Heritage Plan: Distinctly New West includes five platform areas:***

1. **Deliver a Heritage Plan in 18 Months** New Westminster is not starting from scratch. The City has already done years of heritage work, including the Queen's Park Heritage Conservation Area and work to refresh the City's 2011 Heritage Revitalization Agreement (HRA) Policy. That refresh began in 2021 but has been paused since May 2022 due to City staffing constraints, with no revised completion date set.

This work doesn't need to be redone. It needs to move. We will finish the unfinished work, address outstanding issues, and deliver a comprehensive Heritage Plan within our first 18 months, with clear actions and timelines and annual public reporting on progress.

We will continue supporting the Queen's Park Heritage Conservation Area and work with neighbourhoods to establish additional Heritage Conservation Areas where there is demonstrated community support.

2. **Make Heritage Preservation Practical** – New Westminster already has tools to encourage heritage preservation, but some are complicated, unfinished or have been affected by changes in provincial housing legislation.

Through the 18-month Heritage Plan, we will modernize the City's approach to heritage preservation, integrate Heritage considerations into City planning where appropriate, complete unfinished work on Heritage Revitalization Agreements, and identify practical incentives and simpler City processes that make heritage preservation easier.

3. **Put Residents Back at the Table** As development processes change, residents should not lose their voice in decisions affecting our heritage.

Under the province's Bill 44 (Housing Statutes (Residential Development) Amendment Act, 2023), the City can no longer hold public hearings on residential rezonings that are consistent with the Official Community Plan. Where this has removed a public hearing residents would once have had, we will provide meaningful opportunities for residents to have input on heritage features and streetscapes within the City's authority.

The Community Heritage Commission is a separate advisory body from the subject-matter committees being re-established under our governance reforms. We will strengthen its role in providing timely advice to Council and ensure heritage considerations and community input happen early, before important City decisions are too far along.

4. **Heritage Belongs to Everyone** New Westminster's heritage is broad and diverse. It includes Indigenous history and heritage, our buildings and streetscapes, and the people, communities, institutions, traditions and stories that have shaped New Westminster over generations. Through the Heritage Plan, we will ensure our shared history is recognized and carried forward. This includes long-standing civic traditions such as May Day, Hyack and the Anvil Battery salute, cultural institutions such as Royal City Musical Theatre and the New Westminster Symphony Orchestra, community organizations, and the histories and experiences of the many communities, including Indigenous communities, that have made New Westminster home.

5. **Build on Our Heritage as an Economic Asset** New Westminster already has heritage attractions, walking tours, museums, historic neighbourhoods and traditions worth discovering. We will do more to connect and promote them – including working with local heritage, tourism and community partners to develop a made-in-New West self-guided tour of unique heritage sites. This will include integrating and expanding QR codes on public infrastructure to provide easy access to heritage information on key sites throughout the city.

We will develop a coordinated approach to heritage tourism, working with Tourism New Westminster, heritage organizations and local businesses to connect our historic places, streetscapes, cultural institutions, festivals and traditions in ways that attract visitors and support local businesses.

**Growing Without Losing Who We Are**

New Westminster will continue to grow and change. We can welcome new housing and new neighbours while protecting the heritage that makes our city distinctive.

Our history belongs to all of us. Let's make sure it remains part of our future.

# NWP launches Stronger Together plan to build a more caring and prepared New Westminster

## Stronger Together is about supporting community organizations, activating volunteers, ensuring New Westminster is prepared for emergencies, and building stronger neighbourhood connections.

*New Westminster, B.C. (August 4, 2026)* — The New Westminster Progressives have released *Stronger Together*, a community-focused platform to help build a more caring, connected and resilient New Westminster.

The plan starts with a simple idea: strong communities are built when neighbours help neighbours — and when we are ready to face emergencies together.

The New Westminster Progressives believe City Hall has an important role to play — not by taking over responsibilities that belong to senior governments, but by ensuring New Westminster is prepared for emergencies, supporting local organizations, recognizing community leadership, and bringing people together around a common purpose.

"This is about getting City Hall focused on the basics: making sure the city is ready for emergencies, supporting volunteers and backing local organizations that deliver results," says Daniel Fontaine, NWP mayoral candidate. "Stronger Together is a practical plan to support the people, volunteers and local organizations that already make our city work."

The plan includes a modernized community grant program, annual Royal City Community Awards, a Royal City Community Credit Program, Royal City Responds and a Mayor's Community Partnership Table.

"New Westminster has always been strongest when people step up for each other," says Maryann Morrison, NWP council candidate. "That means volunteers, community organizations, first responders, local leaders and the city working together to support people through everyday challenges and extraordinary circumstances. Our plan is about recognizing that work and making it easier for people to stay connected before, during and after emergencies."

***The full NWP Stronger Together plan includes six platform areas:***

1. **Support community organizations that deliver results.** Strong community organizations help build strong neighbourhoods. We will:
   - Modernize the City's community grant program by simplifying applications and reducing unnecessary administrative requirements.
   - Support multi-year funding agreements, where appropriate, to provide greater certainty for community organizations.
   - Continue supporting organizations delivering measurable benefits for youth, seniors, sports, arts, culture, food security, accessibility and neighbourhood development.
   - Reinstate City Council's authority to approve all community grants, ensuring transparency, accountability and public oversight.

2. **Celebrate the people who make New Westminster great.** Our City's greatest strength is the people who quietly make a difference every day. We will:
   - Establish the annual Royal City Community Awards, presented by the Mayor, celebrating the outstanding individuals and organizations who strengthen our community.
   - Invite residents to nominate deserving recipients through an open and transparent public process.
   - Celebrate National Volunteer Week with an annual civic appreciation event recognizing volunteers across New Westminster.
   - Work with local schools to recognize youth leadership and volunteerism.

3. **Build a stronger culture of volunteerism and neighbour-to-neighbour support.** Strong communities are built when neighbours help neighbours. We will:
   - Establish a Royal City Community Credit Program, inspired by successful time-banking models, to recognize volunteerism, neighbour-to-neighbour support and community participation.
   - Enable residents to earn Royal City Community Credits by volunteering, mentoring youth, assisting seniors, participating in neighbourhood projects, community gardens, environmental stewardship initiatives and other community-building activities.
   - Work with Century House, Residents' Associations, community organizations and local businesses to develop a city-wide network that strengthens community connections while reducing social isolation.
   - Explore opportunities for residents to redeem community credits for volunteer assistance and community support, creating a practical network of mutual aid that benefits people of all ages.
   - Integrate the Royal City Community Credit Program into the City's emergency preparedness efforts by activating trained volunteers to check on vulnerable residents, coordinate community assistance and support neighbourhood response during major emergencies and extreme weather events.

4. **Strengthen emergency preparedness and community response.** Preparing today means protecting tomorrow. We will:
   - Develop a comprehensive Community Resilience Strategy to strengthen preparedness for earthquakes, floods, wildfire smoke, extreme heat and other emergencies.
   - Expand neighbourhood emergency preparedness education and training opportunities.
   - Continue investing in emergency equipment, backup power and designated community emergency gathering locations.
   - Launch Royal City Responds – a community emergency response network and online portal that can be activated immediately following major emergencies to more effectively coordinate community donations, volunteers, temporary accommodation, gift cards, food, clothing and other assistance.
   - Establish standing partnerships with local businesses, charitable organizations, faith communities and service clubs so support can be mobilized quickly when residents are displaced by fires or other emergencies.
   - Continue advocating for provincial and federal funding to strengthen emergency preparedness and disaster recovery.

5. **Strengthen community partnerships.** The City's role is to bring people together and advocate for results. We will:
   - Establish a Mayor's Community Partnership Table, bringing together Fraser Health, School District 40, BC Housing, New Westminster Police, Fire & Rescue Services, Indigenous organizations, social service agencies, seniors' organizations, youth organizations, the Chamber of Commerce and Residents' Associations.
   - Meet at least twice annually to improve coordination, identify service gaps and advocate collectively to senior governments.
   - Publish an annual Community Wellbeing Report highlighting progress, emerging issues and community priorities.
   - Continue advocating for senior orders of government to fulfill their primary responsibilities for housing, mental health and addiction services.

6. **Build healthier and more inclusive neighbourhoods.** Healthy communities ensure everyone has the opportunity to participate. We will:
   - Continue supporting age-friendly initiatives and improve accessibility throughout our city.
   - Improve access to cooling centres, emergency information and community support during extreme weather events.
   - Support community gardens, neighbourhood celebrations and initiatives that strengthen connections between neighbours.
   - Continue supporting programs that reduce social isolation and encourage active participation by seniors, youth and newcomers.

**Together, we'll build a stronger New Westminster.**

# NWP ready to govern on Day One with 100-day plan

## 15 actions. 100 days. Progress for a change.

*New Westminster, B.C. (September 20, 2026)* — The New Westminster Progressives are ready to govern.

Today, the NWP launched *Ready to Govern: Our First 100 Days*, a plan setting out 15 actions a NWP Council will begin immediately after being elected, focused on tax relief, safer streets, cleaner neighbourhoods, stronger core services and practical fixes residents can see.

"New Westminster's challenges will not be solved in 100 days, but the direction of City Hall can change on Day One," says Daniel Fontaine, NWP candidate for mayor. "A NWP Council will arrive ready to govern, ready to work with City staff and ready to deliver practical change residents can see."

The first 100 days will include immediate action to cut the 3.5% electricity climate tax, refocus the 2027 budget on affordability and core services, freeze mayor, council and senior executive pay, launch an independent core services review, begin hiring 12 new community enforcement and safety officers, clean up and repair neighbourhoods, restore meaningful citizen advisory committees, cancel the $500,000 rebranding project, move the waterfront pier rebuild forward, strengthen city–schools coordination, cut red tape at City Hall, kickstart the Columbia District, tackle New Westminster's 20 worst traffic bottlenecks, renew action on train noise and redirect $90,000 from the Sue Big Oil class-action lawsuit to local climate action.

"Frontline City employees will be essential to delivering this change," Fontaine adds. "We will work with them to rebuild a City Hall focused on excellent core services, respect for taxpayers and measurable results."

***The plan:***

1. **Cut the 3.5% electricity climate tax.** Deliver immediate relief for New Westminster households and businesses.
2. **Put affordability back into the budget.** Get the 2027 budget underway with a renewed focus on affordability and core services, including an immediate 10% reduction in consultant spending — putting the City on track to cut consultant costs by at least 20% within two years.
3. **Freeze mayor, council and senior executive pay.** Immediately freeze mayor, council and senior executive pay until we get our fiscal house back in order.
4. **Launch an independent core services review.** Determine what City Hall must do, should do and should stop doing — protecting frontline services while finding savings through better processes, technology, attrition and redeployment.
5. **Cancel the Sue Big Oil campaign.** Cancel the city's $90,000 bill to participate in the Sue Big Oil class-action lawsuit, and reinvest savings into local climate action such as urban tree planting and enhanced Queensborough flood protection.
6. **Start hiring 12 new community enforcement and safety officers.** Increase visible foot and bicycle patrols in neighbourhoods and commercial districts.
7. **Clean up and repair our neighbourhoods.** Accelerate graffiti removal and street cleaning, begin installing 100 new waste and recycling receptacles, start priority sidewalk repairs and launch a street-tree planting plan.
8. **Give residents their voice back.** Replace the Community Advisory Assembly with focused, meaningful citizen advisory committees so residents have a real voice before major decisions are made.
9. **Make New West the Royal City again.** Cancel the wasteful $500,000 rebranding project and redirect those resources to priorities residents can see and feel.
10. **Get the waterfront pier rebuild moving.** Put a clear repair plan, construction timeline and funding strategy before Council.
11. **Get the City and schools working together again.** Enhance formal City–Schools coordination so growth, schools, childcare, recreation and community infrastructure are better planned together.
12. **Cut red tape at City Hall.** Launch the Red Tape Reduction Task Force and Permit Concierge to make City Hall easier to navigate for residents, builders and businesses.
13. **Kickstart the Columbia District.** Begin delivering at least 10 new micro-retail opportunities, eliminate patio permit fees and launch a business-led push to attract new restaurants, employers and investment.
14. **Tackle our 20 worst traffic bottlenecks.** Identify the problem locations and begin developing practical fixes involving signal timing, intersections and traffic flow.
15. **Take action on train noise.** Re-establish the Railway Community Advisory Panel and renew the push for additional quiet zones and more livable neighbourhoods.

***By Day 100:***

- Tax relief will have begun.
- A stronger community safety presence will be underway.
- The 2027 budget will be focused on affordability and core services.
- Residents will have a stronger voice at City Hall.
- Work will be moving on traffic, train noise, neighbourhood improvements, local climate action and the waterfront pier.
- The Columbia District revitalization will be underway.

> "We will not accomplish everything in 100 days," Fontaine says. "But nobody will wonder whether change has begun."
